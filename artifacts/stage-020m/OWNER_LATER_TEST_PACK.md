## Stage 020M — Body Dimensions and Relative Scale

1. **Dimension-line readability.** The leaders stand off the body by 30 gizmo
   reference units along the body's own axes. On a real screen, at a real zoom,
   against a real body: are the three annotations separable from one another and
   from the grid, and does the active one read as active?
2. **X / Y / Z label placement on real hardware.** Each label is centred on the
   midpoint of its own dimension line and clamped into the window. Do the three
   collide at some camera angles, and does a clamped label still read as
   belonging to its axis?
3. **All three anchor pictograms.** `ic_anchor_negative`, `ic_anchor_center` and
   `ic_anchor_positive` are a solid held bar, an outlined body and one or two
   arrows. Is "which side stays still" readable at 24 dp without reading the
   content description? **The exact visual icon design is OWNER LATER and no
   approval is claimed.**
4. **Rotated Construction bodies.** The anchor arithmetic is proved exact by
   `DIM020M-04/05/07`. What is not proved is whether growing a turned body away
   from a chosen side *feels* like the right side, seen through a real camera.
5. **Several primitive types.** The domain suite covers all six for the reading;
   the device journey covers one for the editing. A box, a cylinder, a capsule
   and a plane through the real controls is the OWNER's pass.
6. **Exact dimension entry ergonomics.** The label opens a compact field over
   the viewport, seeded and selected whole. Is the field big enough, is the
   keyboard in the way, and is Apply where a thumb expects it?
7. **Relative Scale X/Y/Z workflow.** Three multipliers and one Apply, reset to
   1 every time. Is "multiply by" the right mental model at a real desk, and is
   the reset surprising or reassuring?
8. **Undo / Redo feel.** One exact dimension edit is one Undo and one Relative
   Scale Apply is one Undo. Does taking a resize back land where the user
   expects, especially after a one-sided anchor moved the body?
9. **Interaction with left/right-handed rail and palettes.** The two entries and
   the anchor selector are members of the one trailing host, so handedness moves
   them with it and the five palettes tint them like every other control. Both
   are unverified visually here.
